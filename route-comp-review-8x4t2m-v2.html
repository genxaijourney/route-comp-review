Route Sales Representative — Compensation Review

Current pay of $55,000 sits below the published median for the title. An adjustment to $65,000 lands squarely mid-band for the same role — well inside the $42,515–$76,261 range Salary.com reports. This is a market-rate correction, not an outlier request.

Data-Source Caveat The federal Bureau of Labor Statistics occupation code for "driver / sales workers" (SOC 53-3031) shows a median of $37,130 (May 2024). That code bundles pizza delivery and similar delivery-driver roles in with route sales. It is not the correct comparison. The correct comparison title is "Route Sales Representative."

§ 04  ·  Retention vs. Replacement

The Math for the Employer

Source · Gallup · SHRM

Industry research on turnover cost puts the true expense of replacing a worker at 50% to 200% of that worker's annual salary. Even at the conservative floor, the arithmetic tilts hard toward retention.

Current salary$55,000
Conservative replacement cost (50% of salary)$27,500
SHRM average hard recruiting cost per hire~$4,700
Proposed raise (one-time and recurring)$10,000 / yr
Replacement cost is $27,500 against a $10,000 raise — nearly three times more expensive to lose the role than to retain it.

And that's before route-relationship cost — the goodwill a route sales rep builds at each stop is not a line item on a spreadsheet, but every convenience-store operator knows a bad handoff loses shelf placement and repeat orders.

§ 05  ·  If They Say That, You Say This

Rehearsed Responses to Standard Pushback

Five objections to expect, and the calm, data-anchored response for each. Read them once before the meeting; do not read them from the page during it.

If they say
"We can't afford it right now — times are tight for the industry."
You say
The industry set a record in 2025 — convenience-store in-store sales hit $341.2B and total industry sales reached $817.5B, per NACS. As fuel margins compress, the merchandise side — the side route sales reps drive — is what's growing. A $10,000 adjustment inside a healthy revenue category is not the ask that breaks a budget.
If they say
"You knew the pay when you took the job."
You say
A wage set to a 2016 price level shouldn't be permanent when the price level is up 38.8%. The company doesn't ask its suppliers to hold 2016 wholesale — and its suppliers don't. The wage band is a market-rate obligation, not a lifetime contract signed once.
If they say
"We give a 3% cost-of-living raise every year — that keeps up with inflation."
You say
3% compounded over 10 years lands near +34% — assuming no missed years. CPI is +38.8%. Even the ideal 3% path falls behind the price level. And the ask isn't just to catch up on inflation — it's to align with the published market median for the title.
If they say
"Anyone would take this job for what we're paying — we can replace you."
You say
Replacement isn't free. Even at the conservative floor, industry turnover cost is $27,500 for this salary — nearly three times the raise being asked for. And that assumes the replacement stays, which turnover data on below-market pay suggests is unlikely. The math favors retention.
If they say
"We already pay competitively for the role."
You say
Salary.com's 2026 data for "Route Sales Representative" puts the median at $58,471 and the range at $42,515–$76,261. Current pay sits below the median; the ask lands mid-band. If the company has a different comp study that says otherwise, share it — the conversation can happen off that shared data.
§ 06  ·  Talking Points

What to Actually Say in the Room

60-Second Opener "I've been giving my compensation some thought and I wanted to bring you a market-rate correction, not a personal request. The published median for 'Route Sales Representative' is $58,471; the range tops out around $76,000. I'm currently at $55,000, so I'm below the median for the exact title I hold. An adjustment to $65,000 puts me squarely mid-band. Given prices are up 38.8% since 2016 and turnover cost is roughly three times the raise, I think it's the right conversation to have."
  1. Lead with the market-rate gap, using the correct job title. Salary.com puts the median for "Route Sales Representative" at $58,471. Ask lands mid-band, not above it. If the SOC 53-3031 number comes up, correct it politely — that code includes pizza delivery.
  2. Frame the economy as context for a stale wage band — never as personal need. Prices are up 38.8% since 2016. The wage band was set to a different price level.
  3. Point to the shelves. Coffee is up 118%. Ground beef up 69%. Cigarettes up 70%. Chips up 54%. Every price sticker on the route makes the same case the CPI does.
  4. Close on retention math. A $10,000 raise is roughly one-third of what turnover costs the employer at the conservative floor. The raise is the cheaper option.
  5. Ask for a written response inside two weeks. If the answer is no, ask what specific market data the "no" is based on — and offer to circle back in 90 days with any additional evidence they'd like to see.
Do Not Say Do not frame the ask around needing money, household expenses, family obligations, or any personal circumstance. Do not compare pay to specific coworkers. Do not threaten to quit — retention math makes the point without a threat. The entire case rests on published market data and economy-wide numbers. Anything personal weakens it.
