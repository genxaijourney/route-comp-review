Current pay of $55,000 sits below the published median for the title. An adjustment to $65,000 lands squarely mid-band for the same role — well inside the $42,515–$76,261 range Salary.com reports. This is a market-rate correction, not an outlier request.

Data-Source Caveat The federal Bureau of Labor Statistics occupation code for "driver / sales workers" (SOC 53-3031) shows a median of $37,130 (May 2024). That code bundles pizza delivery and similar delivery-driver roles in with route sales. It is not the correct comparison. The correct comparison title is "Route Sales Representative."

§ 03  ·  Retention vs. Replacement

The Math for the Employer

Source · Gallup · SHRM

Industry research on turnover cost puts the true expense of replacing a worker at 50% to 200% of that worker's annual salary. Even at the conservative floor, the arithmetic tilts hard toward retention.

Current salary $55,000
Conservative replacement cost (50% of salary) $27,500
SHRM average hard recruiting cost per hire ~$4,700
Proposed raise (one-time and recurring) $10,000 / yr
Replacement cost is $27,500 against a $10,000 raise — nearly three times more expensive to lose the role than to retain it.
§ 04  ·  Talking Points

What to Actually Say in the Room

  1. Lead with the market-rate gap, using the correct job title. Salary.com puts the median for "Route Sales Representative" at $58,471. Ask lands mid-band, not above it.
  2. Frame the economy as context for a stale wage band — never as personal need. Prices are up 38.8% since 2016; the wage band was set against a different price level.
  3. Close on retention math. A $10,000 raise is roughly one-third of what turnover costs the employer at the conservative floor. The raise is the cheaper option.
Do Not Say Do not frame the ask around needing money, household expenses, or any personal circumstance. The entire case rests on market data and economy-wide numbers. Anything personal weakens it.
